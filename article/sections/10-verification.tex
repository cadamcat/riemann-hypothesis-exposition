\section{Verification status}\label{sec:verification}

This section states what has been checked about the source, by whom, and what has not. It draws on
two public sources: a joint statement by the Lean FRO and the Mathlib Initiative \cite{leanfro-mathlib}
(\S\ref{sec:joint}), and an independent verification record \cite{sutherland-1255}, as of its update of
21 November 2026, 12:55 UTC (\S\ref{sec:record}). The record says that it will be updated as its
remaining checks finish.

\paragraph{Static scan (done, by us).} A text scan of all 175{,}187 files reports no matches for the
listed source patterns: \texttt{axiom}, \texttt{sorry}, \texttt{admit}, \texttt{native\_decide},
\texttt{implemented\_by}, \texttt{extern}, \texttt{unsafe}, \texttt{\#eval}, \texttt{initialize},
patterns for input/output, process and file calls, and options that skip kernel checking. It
identified the five \texttt{run\_cmd} sites, in the files \texttt{BuildAid/D1}--\texttt{D5}, each
adding one namespace to Lean's library-suggestion deny list \stlx{src/BuildAid/D1.lean}{4}, and the
declaration-copying elaborator in
\texttt{Formalism/HermitianCurvature/HermitianCurvatureProofs/CutCptCopy.lean}, which adds
declarations obtained by substitution from existing ones (through \texttt{addDecl}) and reports
their number. The kernel checks such declarations like any other. The tree contains no local
definition of \mathlib{RiemannHypothesis} or \mathlib{riemannZeta}. A text scan does not establish
kernel acceptance; the checks below address it.

\subsection{The joint statement of the Lean FRO and the Mathlib Initiative}\label{sec:joint}

On 21 November 2026 (13:55 UTC) the Lean FRO and the Mathlib Initiative published a joint statement,
``Lean verification of the Riemann Hypothesis'', on the Lean community blog \cite{leanfro-mathlib}.
It reports that the codebase depends directly only on Mathlib, and it certifies ``that both the
statement and the proof of the Riemann Hypothesis are correct''. It gives four criteria, taken from
a post on the Xena Project blog that it links (\nolinkurl{xenaproject.wordpress.com}, 12 November
2026), and states that all four are satisfied. Table~\ref{tab:criteria} lists them with how the
statement says each is met. For the record of individual checks, the statement points to the
verification record of \S\ref{sec:record}.

\subsection{The verification record}\label{sec:record}

A.~Sutherland (MIT) built the source and ran several kernel checks on it \cite{sutherland-1255}. The
record's source archive has the same SHA-256 hash as the archive this article reads
(\S\ref{sec:reading}), and it uses Lean v4.33.1 and Mathlib v4.33.1 (commit \texttt{0df444a3}). Some
checks run on the compiled modules. Others run on the exported proof (the output of
\texttt{lean4export} on module \texttt{Main}) or compare it with an exported ``challenge'': the five
statements with \texttt{sorry} proofs, importing only Mathlib. In the record's words, con-leche's
\texttt{-{}-compare} mode is ``an independent implementation of Comparator's statement matching''.

\noindent\begin{minipage}{\linewidth}
The statements, as given in the record:
\begin{Verbatim}
def RiemannHypothesis : Prop :=
  ∀ (s : ℂ) (_ : riemannZeta s = 0) (_ : ¬∃ n : ℕ, s = -2 * (n + 1)) (_ : s ≠ 1), s.re = 1 / 2
theorem riemannHypothesis : RiemannHypothesis

theorem riemannZeta_eq_zero_re :
  ∀ s : ℂ, riemannZeta s = 0 → (¬∃ n : ℕ, s = -2 * ((n : ℂ) + 1)) → s ≠ 1 → s.re = 1 / 2

theorem LFunction_ne_zero_of_half_lt_re :
  ∀ (N : ℕ) [NeZero N] (χ : DirichletCharacter ℂ N) (s : ℂ),
    1 / 2 < s.re → (χ ≠ 1 ∨ s ≠ 1) → DirichletCharacter.LFunction χ s ≠ 0

theorem LFunction_eq_zero_re :
  ∀ (N : ℕ) [NeZero N] (χ : DirichletCharacter ℂ N) (s : ℂ),
    0 < s.re → s.re < 1 → DirichletCharacter.LFunction χ s = 0 → s.re = 1 / 2

theorem LFunction_eq_zero_re_of_isPrimitive :
  ∀ (N : ℕ) [NeZero N] (χ : DirichletCharacter ℂ N), χ.IsPrimitive →
    ∀ s : ℂ, 0 < s.re → s.re < 1 → DirichletCharacter.LFunction χ s = 0 → s.re = 1 / 2
\end{Verbatim}
\end{minipage}

The record keeps a scope remark: ``GRH'' there means Dirichlet $L$-functions, for all moduli and all
characters. Dedekind zeta functions, Hecke $L$-functions and automorphic $L$-functions in general are
not among the statements that were checked.

Sutherland built the source on unsandboxed cloud machines by invoking Lean directly with the inputs
that Lake supplies. The checked export came from that build. The complete per-module compiled-file
manifest depends on the build path because Lean v4.33.1 stores absolute source paths with linter
messages. The record identifies the export hash as the cross-build comparison target and leaves
open whether exports can contain source paths. The full comparison of compiled files with a Lake
build and the export-hash comparison with a sandboxed Lake build remain open in the record. The
record adds: ``A passing check shows that the formal statements, as written in Lean against
Mathlib's definitions, follow from the three standard axioms according to the kernels used. It
relies on those kernels and on Mathlib's definitions being what they are intended to be.''

\FloatBarrier
\paragraph{Checks of two divisor-sum inequalities.} The record also combines the proof with
Robin1984 (\texttt{kimihiro64/Robin1984}, commit \texttt{acab1a3}), an independent formalization of
Robin's and Lagarias's criteria on the same Lean and Mathlib versions \cite{sutherland-1255}.
Importing both gives two theorems about the divisor sum, with no hypotheses and no mention of
$\zeta$. As stated in the record:

\noindent\begin{minipage}{\linewidth}
\begin{Verbatim}
theorem robin_inequality :
    ∀ n : ℕ, 5040 < n →
      ((ArithmeticFunction.sigma 1 n : ℕ) : ℝ) <
        Real.exp Real.eulerMascheroniConstant * (n : ℝ) * Real.log (Real.log (n : ℝ))

theorem lagarias_inequality :
    ∀ n : ℕ, 0 < n →
      ((ArithmeticFunction.sigma 1 n : ℕ) : ℝ) ≤
        (harmonic n : ℝ) + Real.exp (harmonic n : ℝ) * Real.log (harmonic n : ℝ)
\end{Verbatim}
\end{minipage}

\noindent That is, $\sigma(n)<e^{\gamma}n\log\log n$ for every $n>5040$, and
$\sigma(n)\le H_n+e^{H_n}\log H_n$ for every $n\ge1$, where $\sigma(n)$ is the sum of the divisors
of $n$ and $H_n$ the $n$-th harmonic number. Classically, each inequality is equivalent to the
Riemann hypothesis (Robin's and Lagarias's criteria) \stcs. Both theorems compile and
\texttt{\#print axioms} reports only \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice} and
\texttt{Quot.sound}. con-leche \texttt{master} accepts the full combined export (1{,}472{,}825
declarations), con-leche \texttt{-{}-compare} accepts both theorems against a challenge file that
imports only Mathlib, and nanoda accepts the combined export. The \texttt{lake comparator} run on
the combined export had passed its statement comparison and axiom check; its Lean kernel stages
were still running. Robin1984 on its own (its three equivalences with the
Riemann hypothesis) passed its build, con-leche with \texttt{-{}-compare}, the unmodified
Comparator v4.33.0 and \texttt{lake comparator} (Lean v4.36.0). In the record's words, ``these
statements still rely on Mathlib's definitions of the divisor sum, Euler's constant, harmonic
numbers, \texttt{exp} and \texttt{log}, and on the Robin1984 proof, which is why that proof was put
through the same checks.''

\subsection{What the joint statement does not report}\label{sec:notreported}

The joint statement is issued in the name of the two organizations and names no individual
signatories. It does not report three things that a reader might look for, and this article does
not supply them by inference.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{An axiom check.} The statement lists none. The verification record does:
  \texttt{\#print axioms} for the five theorems reports \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}
  and \texttt{Quot.sound} only (Table~\ref{tab:verification}).
\item \emph{Identifiers.} The statement gives no hash of the source archive or of the export, and no
  toolchain (Lean or Mathlib version). The record gives both.
\item \emph{A human reading of the argument.} The statement describes none. It closes by looking
  forward to the activity in the mathematical community that will ``help explain the new ideas in
  this proof''.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Status}\label{sec:status}

The theorem \lean{riemannHypothesis}~:~\mathlib{RiemannHypothesis} has a Lean proof that the Lean
FRO and the Mathlib Initiative certify as correct, statement and proof \cite{leanfro-mathlib}, and
that independent checkers accept (\S\S\ref{sec:joint}--\ref{sec:record}). That the theorem states the
Riemann hypothesis is addressed by the statement's criterion~1 (expert vetting of Mathlib's
definition) and criterion~3 (the comparison tools); the Robin--Lagarias check of \S\ref{sec:record}
is independent support, through statements that do not mention $\zeta$. A \sttag{Lean} label marks a
statement in the source; the proof is certified as reported in this section. What remains is the
mathematical community's reading and explanation of the argument, which the statement itself looks
forward to. This article maps and explains the argument; it does not judge it.
