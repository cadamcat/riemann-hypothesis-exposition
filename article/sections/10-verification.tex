\section{Verification status}\label{sec:verification}

This section states what has been checked about the source, by whom, and what has not. The kernel
results come from one independent verification record \cite{sutherland}, as of its update of
21 November 2026, 09:30 UTC. The record says that it will be updated as its remaining checks
finish.

\paragraph{Static scan (done, by us).} A text scan of all 175{,}187 files reports no matches for the
listed source patterns: \texttt{axiom}, \texttt{sorry}, \texttt{admit}, \texttt{native\_decide},
\texttt{implemented\_by}, \texttt{extern}, \texttt{unsafe}, \texttt{\#eval}, \texttt{initialize},
patterns for input/output, process and file calls, and options that skip kernel checking. It
identified the five \texttt{run\_cmd} sites, in the files \texttt{BuildAid/D1}--\texttt{D5}, each
adding one namespace to Lean's library-suggestion deny list \stlx{src/BuildAid/D1.lean}{4}, and the
declaration-copying elaborator in
\texttt{Formalism/HermitianCurvature/HermitianCurvatureProofs/CutCptCopy.lean}, which adds
declarations obtained by substitution from existing ones (through \texttt{addDecl}) and reports
their number. The kernel checks such declarations like any other. The tree contains no local
definition of \mathlib{RiemannHypothesis} or \mathlib{riemannZeta}. A text scan does not establish
kernel acceptance; the checks below address it.

\paragraph{The verification record.} A.~Sutherland (MIT) built the source and ran several kernel
checks on it \cite{sutherland}. The record's source archive has the same SHA-256 hash as the archive
this article reads (\S\ref{sec:reading}), and it uses Lean v4.33.1 and Mathlib v4.33.1 (commit
\texttt{0df444a3}). Some checks run on the compiled modules. Others run on the exported proof (the
output of \texttt{lean4export} on module \texttt{Main}) or compare it with an exported
``challenge'': the five statements with \texttt{sorry} proofs, importing only Mathlib. In the
record's words, con-leche's \texttt{-{}-compare} mode is ``an independent implementation of
Comparator's statement matching''. Table~\ref{tab:verification} lists the checks and their results
at that time.

At that time two of the record's entries for the main proof were still running: the Lean default
and paranoid kernels on the export, and the end-to-end \texttt{lake comparator} run. Three older binaries gave no verdict: the
con-leche and con-ron bundled with Lean v4.36.0 declined (exit 2, not a rejection), because they do
not support one reflexive inductive type in the proof, and the lean4lean bundled with Lean v4.36.0
cannot read a v4.33 core library.

The record names the verified theorem: \lean{riemannHypothesis}~:~\mathlib{RiemannHypothesis} in
\texttt{Main.lean}, where \mathlib{RiemannHypothesis} is Mathlib's own definition, and four further
theorems, among them the analogous statement for all Dirichlet $L$-functions. In the record,
``GRH'' means Dirichlet $L$-functions only.

Sutherland built the source on unsandboxed cloud machines by invoking Lean directly with the inputs
that Lake supplies. The checked export came from that build. The complete per-module compiled-file
manifest depends on the build path because Lean v4.33.1 stores absolute source paths with linter
messages. The record identifies the export hash as the cross-build comparison target and leaves
open whether exports can contain source paths. The full comparison of compiled files with a Lake
build and the export-hash comparison with a sandboxed Lake build remain open. The record adds: ``A passing
check shows that the formal statements, as written in Lean against Mathlib's definitions, follow
from the three standard axioms according to the kernels used. It relies on those kernels and on
Mathlib's definitions being what they are intended to be.''

\FloatBarrier
\paragraph{Checks of two divisor-sum inequalities.} The record also combines the proof with
Robin1984 (\texttt{kimihiro64/Robin1984}, commit \texttt{acab1a3}), an independent formalization of
Robin's and Lagarias's criteria on the same Lean and Mathlib versions \cite{sutherland}. Importing
both gives two theorems about the divisor sum, with no hypotheses and no mention of $\zeta$. As
stated in the record:

\noindent\begin{minipage}{\linewidth}
\begin{Verbatim}
theorem robin_inequality :
    ∀ n : ℕ, 5040 < n →
      ((ArithmeticFunction.sigma 1 n : ℕ) : ℝ) <
        Real.exp Real.eulerMascheroniConstant * (n : ℝ) * Real.log (Real.log (n : ℝ))

theorem lagarias_inequality :
    ∀ n : ℕ, 0 < n →
      ((ArithmeticFunction.sigma 1 n : ℕ) : ℝ) ≤
        (harmonic n : ℝ) + Real.exp (harmonic n : ℝ) * Real.log (harmonic n : ℝ)
\end{Verbatim}
\end{minipage}

\noindent That is, $\sigma(n)<e^{\gamma}n\log\log n$ for every $n>5040$, and
$\sigma(n)\le H_n+e^{H_n}\log H_n$ for every $n\ge1$, where $\sigma(n)$ is the sum of the divisors
of $n$ and $H_n$ the $n$-th harmonic number. Classically, each inequality is equivalent to the
Riemann hypothesis (Robin's and Lagarias's criteria) \stcs. Both theorems compile and
\texttt{\#print axioms} reports only \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice} and
\texttt{Quot.sound}. con-leche \texttt{master} accepts the full combined export (1{,}472{,}825
declarations), con-leche \texttt{-{}-compare} accepts both theorems against a challenge file that
imports only Mathlib, and nanoda accepts the combined export. The \texttt{lake comparator} run on
the combined export had passed its statement comparison and axiom check; its Lean kernel stages
were still running. Robin1984 on its own (its three equivalences with the
Riemann hypothesis) passed its build, con-leche with \texttt{-{}-compare}, the unmodified
Comparator v4.33.0 and \texttt{lake comparator} (Lean v4.36.0). In the record's words, ``these
statements still rely on Mathlib's definitions of the divisor sum, Euler's constant, harmonic
numbers, \texttt{exp} and \texttt{log}, and on the Robin1984 proof, which is why that proof was put
through the same checks.''

\paragraph{Other public verification efforts.} Not surveyed in this article.

\paragraph{Status.} The source's theorem \lean{riemannHypothesis}~:~\mathlib{RiemannHypothesis}
(Mathlib's definition) has been machine-checked by several independent checkers, as reported in the
record \cite{sutherland}. A \sttag{Lean} label identifies a location in the Lean source. The record
reports kernel replay of every compiled module. What
remains is the reading of the mathematical argument by experts, and the agreement of Mathlib's
definitions with the classical ones; the Robin--Lagarias check above bears on the second. This
article maps and explains the argument; it does not judge it.

\paragraph{Context.} On 20 November 2026 Ravi Vakil posted on his blog an independent,
human-written manuscript of about 940 pages by Alessandro Masullo, claiming a proof of the
generalized Riemann hypothesis (GRH) \cite{vakil-masullo}. Brian Conrad had reviewed it for months.
Status: claimed. This article does not discuss the manuscript.
